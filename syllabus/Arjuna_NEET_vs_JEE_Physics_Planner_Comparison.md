# Arjuna NEET 2027 vs Arjuna JEE 2027 Physics Planner

## Documents compared

- **Arjuna NEET 2027 Physics:** `037c4bef-ae76-4a90-a1a0-92ab91a8c39c.pdf`
- **Arjuna JEE 2027 Physics:** `efbb3de5-0932-4065-a8c3-f0d176bc94ad.pdf`

## Main conclusion

For a student targeting **CBSE Class 11 plus NEET**, the **Arjuna NEET planner is the better primary course**. It is shorter, follows NEET-oriented depth, gives more balanced time to thermal physics and waves, and finishes one week earlier.

The JEE planner is substantially deeper in mechanics. It can be useful selectively for difficult mechanics chapters, but following the complete JEE planner would add unnecessary workload for a NEET-focused Class 11 student and would delay syllabus completion.

## Overall comparison

### Arjuna NEET 2027

- Starts: **13 April 2026**
- Ends: **29 January 2027**
- Total Physics lectures: **149**
- Faculty: **Saleem Ahmad Sir**
- Main emphasis: NCERT/NEET concepts, standard NEET applications and selected advanced examples

### Arjuna JEE 2027

- Starts: **13 April 2026**
- Ends: **5 February 2027**
- Total Physics lectures: **173**
- Faculty: **Rajwant Singh Sir**
- Main emphasis: deeper mechanics, advanced applications and JEE-level problem varieties

### Difference

- JEE has **24 more lectures** than NEET.
- JEE contains about **16% more lectures** than the NEET planner.
- NEET completes the syllabus **one week earlier**.
- Neither planner shows a dedicated full revision period before completion.

# Chapter-wise lecture-count comparison

## Mathematical Tools

- NEET: **12 lectures**
- JEE: **12 lectures**
- Difference: Same count.
- NEET begins with Mathematical Tools; JEE teaches Units first and then Mathematical Tools.

## Vectors

- NEET: **9 separate lectures**, with some vector material repeated in Motion in a Straight Line
- JEE: Included mainly inside Motion in a Plane rather than as a separate chapter
- NEET advantage: Stronger early foundation before kinematics.

## Units and Measurements

- NEET: **10 lectures**
- JEE: **13 lectures**
- JEE adds more repetition and depth in dimensional constants, variables, analysis and limitations.
- NEET explicitly includes Vernier calipers and screw gauge, which helps with NEET Experimental Skills and CBSE practical understanding.

## Motion in a Straight Line

- NEET: **12 lectures**
- JEE: **16 lectures**
- JEE devotes six listed lectures to one-dimensional relative motion.
- NEET includes the standard NEET/NCERT treatment and some repeated vector preparation.

## Motion in a Plane

- NEET: **10 lectures**
- JEE: **17 lectures**
- Common: projectile motion, relative motion and circular-motion kinematics.
- JEE adds greater depth in inclined-plane projectiles, change of axes, radius of curvature and advanced projectile cases.
- NEET includes rain–man and river–boat relative-motion applications.

## Laws of Motion and Friction

- NEET: **13 lectures**
- JEE: **17 lectures**
- Common: Newton’s laws, free-body diagrams, connected bodies, pulleys, friction and banking.
- JEE spends more time on pseudo-force questions and constraint relations.
- NEET covers rocket propulsion, angle of friction, angle of repose and standard circular-dynamics applications.

## Circular Motion

- NEET: Included within Motion in a Plane and Laws of Motion
- JEE: **10 additional dedicated lectures**
- JEE covers circular motion much more deeply, including conical pendulum and repeated banking problems.
- For NEET, the complete JEE circular-motion block is usually unnecessary.

## Work, Energy and Power

- NEET: **9 lectures**
- JEE: **12 lectures**
- Both cover variable force, work–energy theorem, potential energy, conservation, power and vertical circular motion.
- JEE gives more time to advanced spring, incline and vertical-circle problems.

## Centre of Mass, System of Particles and Collisions

- NEET: **10 lectures**
- JEE: **15 lectures**
- JEE devotes considerably more time to cavity cases, collision frames, relative kinetic energy and repeated collision problem-solving.
- NEET covers centre of mass, centre of gravity, head-on and oblique collisions at sufficient NEET depth.

## Rotational Motion

- NEET: **14 lectures**
- JEE: **19 lectures**
- Both cover moment of inertia, axis theorems, torque, angular momentum, rotational energy and rolling.
- JEE adds more complex moment-of-inertia, hinge reaction, toppling, slipping and rolling-on-incline cases.
- JEE is useful only as an optional source for stronger mechanics practice.

## Gravitation

- NEET: **5 lectures**
- JEE: **7 lectures**
- Both cover gravitation, variation of gravity, Kepler’s laws and satellites.
- NEET explicitly includes field, potential, escape velocity, orbital velocity, satellite period, satellite energy and weightlessness.
- JEE uses more lectures but its listed planner description is less detailed.

## Mechanical Properties of Solids

- NEET: **2 lectures**
- JEE: **1 lecture**
- NEET gives better visible coverage of stress, strain, elastic moduli, composite rods, hysteresis and beam depression.
- Both allocations are compact; CBSE written practice should be added.

## Mechanical Properties of Fluids

- NEET: **11 lectures**
- JEE: **12 lectures**
- Both cover pressure, fluid flow, Bernoulli’s theorem, viscosity and surface tension.
- NEET explicitly covers buoyancy, Archimedes’ principle, flotation, Poiseuille flow and capillarity.
- JEE adds accelerated/rotating containers, Venturimeter and Pitot tube applications.

## Thermal Properties of Matter

- NEET: **11 lectures**
- JEE: **6 lectures**
- NEET is substantially more complete and includes temperature, expansion, calorimetry, latent heat, conduction, convection, radiation, black-body ideas, Wien’s law, Stefan’s law and Newton’s law of cooling.
- JEE compresses the chapter and focuses strongly on thermal-conduction cases.
- NEET planner is better for CBSE and NEET coverage.

## Kinetic Theory and Thermodynamics

- NEET: **3 Kinetic Theory + 4 Thermodynamics = 7 lectures**
- JEE: **5 combined lectures**
- NEET separately covers gas laws, molecular speeds, equipartition, mean free path, thermodynamic processes, second law, heat engines and Carnot cycle.
- JEE is more compressed and adds polytropic-process and mixture-of-gases applications.

## Oscillations

- NEET: **7 lectures**
- JEE: **6 lectures**
- Both cover SHM graphs, spring systems and pendulums.
- NEET explicitly covers SHM equations, energy, compound/torsional pendulums and liquid-column/submerged-body oscillations.
- JEE includes combination of SHMs.

## Waves

- NEET: **7 lectures**
- JEE: **5 lectures**
- NEET covers wave characteristics, string waves, sound speed, superposition, interference, beats, reflection, stationary waves, strings and organ pipes.
- JEE covers the core topics but in fewer lectures.
- NEET provides better visible alignment with the official NEET and CBSE Waves syllabus.

# Suitability for CBSE Class 11 annual examinations

Both planners teach entrance-examination Physics rather than following the exact pace of a school syllabus.

Important risks:

- Arjuna NEET finishes Waves on **29 January 2027**.
- Arjuna JEE finishes Waves on **5 February 2027**.
- A school annual examination may begin in February.
- Neither planner provides a clear full-syllabus revision block after completing Waves.
- CBSE derivation writing, NCERT exercises, practical work and school-style tests must be added separately.

For the previously selected goal of completing the syllabus and first revision by **15 January 2027**, neither original batch calendar is early enough.

# Recommendation

## Primary course

Use **Arjuna NEET 2027** as the primary content source.

## Optional JEE lectures

Use selected Arjuna JEE recordings only if additional help is needed in:

- Free-body diagrams and constraint relations
- Circular motion
- Work–energy problems
- Centre of mass and collisions
- Rotational motion

Do not automatically watch every JEE lecture, especially repeated pseudo-force, collision, projectile and rolling sessions.

## Required schedule adjustment

To protect the Class 11 annual examination:

1. Follow the NEET topic sequence.
2. Compress repeated advanced problem sessions.
3. Finish core theory by late December.
4. Complete the first full revision by 15 January.
5. Add NCERT examples, derivations, exercises, practical concepts and CBSE written tests every week.

## Final choice

- **For NEET + CBSE:** Arjuna NEET is recommended.
- **For JEE Main/Advanced preparation:** Arjuna JEE is more suitable.
- **For the student’s current target:** Use Arjuna NEET plus selective JEE mechanics support and the accelerated January-safe schedule.
