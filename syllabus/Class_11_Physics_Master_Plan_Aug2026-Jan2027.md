# Class 11 Physics Master Plan

## CBSE 2026–27 + NEET Foundation

**Programme dates:** 24 August 2026–31 January 2027  
**Teaching days:** Monday–Saturday  
**Frequency:** 6 lectures per week  
**Duration:** 60 minutes per lecture  
**Total planned lectures:** 138  
**Core syllabus completion:** 26 December 2026  
**Revision and mock phase:** 28 December 2026–30 January 2027  

This plan is designed specifically for a Class 11 student targeting both the CBSE annual examination and NEET. It is not intended to imitate a JEE batch. Its advantage is better alignment, earlier completion, regular testing, experimental skills and a protected five-week revision period.

## Programme allocation

- Core concepts and chapter problem-solving: **102 lectures**
- Tests, revision, experimental skills, mocks and buffers: **36 lectures**
- Mathematical bridge: included before formal mechanics
- Weekly retrieval quizzes: included inside regular lectures
- Full-syllabus completion occurs more than one month before a likely February annual examination.

## Why this plan is stronger for this student

1. It finishes new theory by December instead of late January or February.
2. It combines CBSE derivations and written presentation with NEET MCQs.
3. It avoids excessive JEE-only repetition in pseudo forces, inclined projectiles, collisions and rolling.
4. It includes spaced revision instead of postponing revision until the syllabus ends.
5. It includes measurement and experimental skills required by NEET and CBSE.
6. It uses test data and an error log to decide remediation.

# Teaching method

## Standard 60-minute concept lecture

- 10 minutes: review priority errors from the previous daily test
- 30 minutes: concept development and derivation
- 15 minutes: guided numerical problems
- 5 minutes: summary and daily-test instructions

## Standard practice lecture

- 10 minutes: formula and concept recall
- 20 minutes: CBSE written questions
- 20 minutes: timed NEET MCQs
- 10 minutes: error analysis

## Daily Test and Feedback Cycle

After every lecture, the student completes a short test before the next class.

### Daily-test design

- 5–10 questions
- Approximately 10–15 minutes
- Low-pressure and diagnostic rather than heavily graded
- About 70% questions from the current topic
- About 30% spaced-revision questions from earlier topics
- Mix of concepts, formulas, calculations and applications

### Next-day review

Before beginning a new topic, the teacher reviews the test analytics and solves the highest-priority questions for 5–10 minutes. The teacher does not need to solve every question again; discussion focuses on:

- Questions missed by many students
- Common misconceptions
- Repeated formula or calculation errors
- Questions where students took unusually long
- Prerequisite gaps that could affect the new topic

### Teacher-side data tracking

Every attempt is recorded and analysed by student, chapter, topic and question. The teacher dashboard should track:

- Test completion
- Score and accuracy
- Topic-wise mastery
- Correct and incorrect responses
- Time taken, when available
- Repeated error patterns
- Improvement across tests
- Strong and weak topics

Students with low completion or repeated topic weakness receive targeted practice or remediation. Daily tests are used to guide teaching, not to punish or publicly rank students.

## Chapter-completion standard

A chapter is considered complete only when the student has:

- Read the relevant NCERT chapter
- Completed NCERT examples and exercises
- Completed the assigned daily topic tests
- Prepared the formula and derivation sheet
- Attempted at least one chapter test
- Corrected all test errors within 48 hours
- Achieved at least 70% before progressing without remediation

# Assessment system

## Daily

- One 5–10 question test after every lecture
- Completion required before the next class
- Teacher reviews topic-wise and question-wise analytics
- Priority errors solved before starting new content
- One weekly recovery opportunity for a legitimately missed test

## Weekly

- Six short daily tests
- One timed NEET MCQ set
- One CBSE written response or derivation
- Daily-test completion, homework and error-log check
- Topic-wise teacher analysis

## Every three to four weeks

- Cumulative test containing older chapters
- Accuracy, speed and presentation scored separately
- Remediation lecture assigned from test evidence

## Final assessment phase

- One full diagnostic examination
- Two sectional tests
- One NEET-style Class 11 mock
- One CBSE full-syllabus mock
- Two targeted weak-chapter clinics

# Student performance dashboard

Track these values every Saturday:

1. Lecture attendance percentage
2. Daily-test completion percentage
3. Homework completion percentage
4. Topic-wise mastery and accuracy
5. MCQ accuracy percentage
6. CBSE written-test percentage
7. Average response time, when available
8. Number of unresolved errors

## Intervention rules

- Below 70% chapter score: mandatory remediation within seven days
- Repeated low daily-test scores: teacher-side topic review and targeted practice
- Missing daily tests: use the weekly recovery opportunity
- Below 80% attendance: use recording/catch-up slot before new content
- More than 10 unresolved errors: pause additional reference-book questions
- Two weak tests in the same chapter: reteach the chapter’s core model problems

# Resource priority

1. NCERT textbook and examples
2. NCERT exercises
3. Teacher’s structured notes and formula sheets
4. NEET chapter-wise MCQs
5. CBSE sample written questions
6. Selected advanced/JEE problems only where they strengthen a syllabus concept

# Final recommendation

The complete programme should end on **31 January 2027**, but new theory must end by **26 December 2026**. January must remain protected for revision, mocks, practical concepts and correction of weaknesses. This structure is more suitable for a CBSE + NEET student than simply extending advanced lectures until February.
