## How to use this script

- Page 1 of this PDF is this guide. After that, **PDF page = deck slide + 1** (Slide 10 is PDF page 11). Each of those pages is one deck slide. The heading is the deck title.
- **Say** is the spoken line. Read it in full language, not in short form.
- **Write** is what you add on the empty board. The slide already shows the printed part.
- **Wait** means do not click next. Let them try.
- **Private** is the answer or the trap. Never read a Private block while the question slide is up.
- Speak **cosec**, never “csc”.
- On a Question slide, do not tick an option and do not flash the solution.
- Tonight slides: assign the work. Do not solve them in class. The answers are in Private only so you are ready after class.

### Before they sit

- Stylus ready. Empty board is for you.
- Presenter View on, this PDF on the side.
- Daily-test reminder at the end, not at the start.

## Slide 3 — Name the sides from θ

**On the board:** right triangle, P orange, B blue, H green, 90° at the left, θ at the base.

### Say

“Every trig ratio is named from one angle. That angle is theta. Do not name the sides from how the figure sits on the page. Name them from theta.”

“The side opposite theta is the perpendicular. We write P.  
The side next to theta, that is not the hypotenuse, is the base. We write B.  
The longest side, opposite the right angle, is the hypotenuse. We write H.”

“If I rotate this triangle, P is still opposite theta. That is the trap. Students lose marks because they call the bottom side the base even when theta has moved.”

### Write

Point at each side as you say P, B, H. If needed, redraw the same triangle standing on a different side and relabel from theta.

### Ask

“If I put theta at the top angle instead, which side becomes P?”

Wait. They should point to the side opposite that new theta.

### Transition

“Now the six ratios. First pair: sine and cosecant.”

## Slide 4 — sin θ and cosec θ

**On the board:** sin θ = P/H, cosec θ = H/P, product = 1.

### Say

“Sine of theta is perpendicular over hypotenuse. Opposite over hypotenuse. Write it as sin theta equals P over H.”

“Cosecant is the reciprocal. Hypotenuse over perpendicular. If sine is 3/5, then cosecant is 5/3.”

“Their product is 1. Sine times cosecant equals 1. That is always true whenever sine is defined and not zero.”

“These are ratios. They have no unit. Never write sine theta equals 0.6 metre.”

### Write

`sin θ = P/H`  
`cosec θ = H/P`  
`sin θ · cosec θ = 1`

One example: `sin θ = 3/5  →  cosec θ = 5/3`

### Transition

“Same pattern for cosine and secant.”

---

## Slide 5 — cos θ and sec θ

**On the board:** cos θ = B/H, sec θ = H/B, product = 1.

### Say

“Cosine of theta is base over hypotenuse. Adjacent over hypotenuse.”

“Secant is the reciprocal. If cosine is 4/5, secant is 5/4.”

“Cosine times secant equals 1. Again, no unit. Never write cosine theta equals 0.8 metre.”

### Write

`cos θ = B/H`  
`sec θ = H/B`  
`cos θ · sec θ = 1`

### Transition

“Last pair: tangent and cotangent.”

---

## Slide 6 — tan θ and cot θ

**On the board:** tan θ = P/B, cot θ = B/P, product = 1.

### Say

“Tangent of theta is perpendicular over base. Opposite over adjacent. This is the ratio we use most in height and distance.”

“Cotangent is the reciprocal. Also remember: tangent is sine over cosine.”

“Three products to lock: sine times cosecant is 1, cosine times secant is 1, tangent times cotangent is 1.”

### Write

`tan θ = P/B`  
`cot θ = B/P`  
`tan θ = sin θ / cos θ`  
and the three product lines.

### Transition

“Next we get the identities. We do not memorise them as three separate miracles. All three come from Pythagoras.”

---

## Slide 7 — Divide Pythagoras by H²

**On the board:** P² + B² = H² divided by H² → sin²θ + cos²θ = 1.

### Say

“Start from the right triangle. Perpendicular squared plus base squared equals hypotenuse squared.”

“Divide every term by H squared. Then P over H is sine, B over H is cosine, H over H is 1.”

“So sine squared plus cosine squared equals 1. This is the identity you keep even if you forget the other two.”

“From this you can rearrange: cosine is the square root of 1 minus sine squared, and sine is the square root of 1 minus cosine squared. For an acute angle we take the positive root.”

### Write

The three lines of the division, then box `sin²θ + cos²θ = 1`.

### Transition

“Same starting line. Now divide by B squared.”

---

## Slide 8 — Divide Pythagoras by B²

**On the board:** 1 + tan²θ = sec²θ.

### Say

“Same Pythagoras. Divide every term by B squared.”

“P over B is tangent. B over B is 1. H over B is secant. So 1 plus tangent squared equals secant squared.”

### Write

The division, then box `1 + tan²θ = sec²θ`.

### Transition

“Once more. Divide by P squared.”

---

## Slide 9 — Divide Pythagoras by P²

**On the board:** 1 + cot²θ = cosec²θ.

### Say

“Divide by P squared. B over P is cotangent. H over P is cosecant. So 1 plus cotangent squared equals cosecant squared.”

“If your mind can hold only one identity in a paper, hold sine squared plus cosine squared equals 1. The other two are the same Pythagoras with a different division.”

### Write

Box `1 + cot²θ = cosec²θ`.

### Transition

“First question. You write. I wait.”

“Copy the triangle. Do not wait for me to write the answer.”

### Wait

## Slide 11 — Solution: First find H

### Say

“Pythagoras. 1 squared plus 2 squared equals x squared. 1 plus 4 equals x squared. x equals square root of 5. We take the positive root because a length is positive.”

### Write

`1² + 2² = x²`  
`x = √5`

### Transition

“Now the three ratios.”

## Slide 12 — Solution: Now sin θ, cos θ, tan θ

### Say

“Sine is P over H, so 1 over root 5. Cosine is B over H, so 2 over root 5. Tangent is P over B, so 1 over 2.”

“Check: sine over cosine should be tangent. 1 over root 5, divided by 2 over root 5, is 1 over 2. Same. That check takes ten seconds and catches a wrong ratio.”

### Write

The three ratio lines. Circle tan θ = 1/2.

## Slide 13 — Question: If tan θ = 2

**On the board:** If tan θ = 2, find P, B, H, sin θ and cos θ. θ acute.

### Say

“If tangent theta equals 2, then find the perpendicular, the base and the hypotenuse. Also find sine theta and cosine theta. Take theta acute.”

“You are not given a number for the base. So the sides will have a scale k. That is correct. The ratios will cancel k.”

### Wait

Do not hint k yet unless the room is frozen. Then: “Tangent is 2 over 1. Let the 1 be k.”

### Private

P = 2k, B = k, H = √5 k, sin θ = 2/√5, cos θ = 1/√5.

---

## Slide 14 — Solution: tan θ = 2 → find P, B, H

### Say

“Tangent is P over B equals 2 over 1. Let B equal k. Then P equals 2k. Hypotenuse squared is 4k squared plus k squared, which is 5k squared. Hypotenuse is root 5 times k. Positive, because theta is acute.”

### Write

`P = 2k,  B = k,  H = √5 k`

---

## Slide 15 — Solution: Now sin θ and cos θ

### Say

“Sine is 2k over root 5 k. The k cancels. Sine is 2 over root 5. Cosine is k over root 5 k, so 1 over root 5.”

“A ratio only fixes the shape. The scale always cancels in sine, cosine and tangent. That is why a question can give you only tan theta equals 2.”

### Write

`sin θ = 2/√5`  
`cos θ = 1/√5`

---

## Slide 16 — Question: Range of sin θ

**On the board:** If sin θ = 2, find cos θ and tan θ.

### Say

“If sine theta equals 2, then find the value of cosine theta and tangent theta.”

Read it as a normal question. Do not smile. Do not say trap. Do not say impossible.

### Wait

Let them try the identity. Many will write cosine squared equals 1 minus 4.

### Private

Impossible. −1 ≤ sin θ ≤ 1. Do not click until they have tried.

## Slide 17 — Solution: Method 1 — geometry

### Say

“Before you open the identity, ask: can sine equal 2?”

“Sine is P over H. H is the longest side, so H is greater than P. A longer side over a shorter side cannot be 2. P over H is at most 1.”

“There is no such triangle. Sine theta equals 2 is impossible. Cosine and tangent are not defined for this given.”

### Write

`H > P  ⇒  P/H ≤ 1`  
`sin θ = 2 is impossible`

---

## Slide 18 — Solution: Method 2 — identity

### Say

“If someone still uses the identity: sine squared plus cosine squared equals 1, put 2, get 4 plus cosine squared equals 1, cosine squared equals minus 3. That is not a real number. Same conclusion.”

“Lock the range. Sine lies between minus 1 and 1, including the ends. Cosine the same. Tangent has no finite range. Tangent 90° is not defined.”

### Write

`−1 ≤ sin θ ≤ 1`  
`−1 ≤ cos θ ≤ 1`  
`tan 90° not defined`

### Transition

“Next question is algebra. You will turn a sine–cosine fraction into tangent.”

## Slide 19 — Question: Find tan θ from a sin–cos fraction

### Say

“If sine plus cosine, over sine minus cosine, equals 11 over 5, then find the value of tangent theta.”

“Do not find sine and cosine separately. Aim for sine over cosine.”

### Wait

### Private

tan θ = 8/3.

## Slide 20 — Solution: Cross multiply, then expand

### Say

“Cross multiply. 5 times sine plus cosine equals 11 times sine minus cosine.”

“Open the brackets. 5 sine plus 5 cosine equals 11 sine minus 11 cosine.”

“Next slide we collect. Do not jump.”

### Write

`5(sin θ + cos θ) = 11(sin θ − cos θ)`  
`5 sin θ + 5 cos θ = 11 sin θ − 11 cos θ`

---

## Slide 21 — Solution: Collect, then divide

### Say

“Bring cosine terms to the left, sine terms to the right. 5 cosine plus 11 cosine is 16 cosine. 11 sine minus 5 sine is 6 sine.”

“16 cosine equals 6 sine. Divide both sides by cosine, and swap: sine over cosine equals 16 over 6, which is 8 over 3.”

“Tangent theta is 8 over 3.”

### Write

`16 cos θ = 6 sin θ`  
`tan θ = 8/3`

## Slide 27 — Solution: Nearer tower

### Say

“Nearer tower. Opposite is height h1. Adjacent is x. Tangent theta equals h1 over x. So h1 equals x tan theta.”

### Write

`tan θ = h₁ / x`  
`h₁ = x tan θ`

---

## Slide 28 — Solution: Farther tower, then the difference

### Say

“Farther tower. Adjacent is now d minus x. Elevation is phi. So h2 equals (d minus x) tan phi.”

“Difference, nearer minus farther: x tan theta minus (d minus x) tan phi. That is option 2.”

“If the question asks for the farther minus the nearer, flip the sign. Always sketch before you write tan.”

### Write

`h₂ = (d − x) tan φ`  
`h₁ − h₂ = x tan θ − (d − x) tan φ`

## Slide 29 — Question: From the top of one tower — depression

**On the board:** two towers, distance D, depression θ from the top of h1 to the top of h2. h1 > h2. Four options.

### Say

“Two towers are separated by a horizontal distance D. From the top of the first tower, height h1, the angle of depression to the top of the second tower, height h2, is theta. Which relation correctly gives D in terms of h1, h2 and theta? Take h1 greater than h2.”

“Depression looks down. The opposite side is the drop in height, h1 minus h2, never the sum.”

### Write

Two towers of different heights. Horizontal from the top of the taller to a point above the shorter. The drop h1 − h2 and the run D.

### Wait

### Private

Option 2: D = (h₁ − h₂) / tan θ.

## Slide 30 — Solution: Write tan θ first

### Say

“From the top of the first tower, look down to the top of the second. Opposite is the height drop h1 minus h2. Adjacent is D. Tangent theta equals (h1 minus h2) over D.”

### Write

`tan θ = (h₁ − h₂) / D`

---

## Slide 31 — Solution: Solve for D

### Say

“Rearrange. D equals (h1 minus h2) over tan theta.”

“Wrong options add the heights, or multiply by tan theta. Adding heights would be as if one tower stood on top of the other. That is not this figure.”

### Write

`D = (h₁ − h₂) / tan θ`

## Slide 39 — Solution: From 0° to 90°

## Slide 58 — Tonight: Wire from the top of a pole

### Say

“A pole of height h has a wire attached from the top to the ground at an angle theta. If the wire breaks and falls to the ground without changing length, find the horizontal distance x from the base of the pole where the wire touches the ground.”

Wire length = h / sin θ. After it falls, the free end is that far from the top’s ground-point; distance from the base of the pole is x = h cot θ. Option 1.
